The Missing Millions

Why Young Voters Are Walking Away From British Elections

In 2024, the number of young people who participated in elections fell to levels that had not been seen in ten years. A widening gap between engaged and disengaged British citizens is being revealed by data.

Research conducted by United Ballots in July 2026,the percentage of people aged 18 to 24 who participated in the general election in 2024 was lower than the 47% who did so in 2019. Inconsistently, it remained at home, and the difference is due to its age.


A total of 59.7% of registered voters participated in the general election held in 2024, as reported by the House of Commons Library. This represents the lowest turnout since 2001 and a 7.6% decrease from the previous year. Taking into account all adults, rather than just those who are registered to vote, makes the picture more stark. The analysis conducted by IPPR titled "Half of Us" discovered that only fifty percent of adults in the United Kingdom cast their ballots, which is the lowest percentage since the implementation of universal suffrage. Only three out of every five registered voters actually cast their ballots. According to IPPR, if they had represented themselves as a party, non-voters would have been the largest party in the country.


Across all generations, there was no uniform distribution of anything. Ipsos, a company that has forecasted voting patterns at every general election since 1979, conducted a post-election survey and discovered that the voting patterns of people over the age of 55 were comparable in 2024 as they were in 2019. The number of voters under the age of 55, particularly younger voters, decreased. The majority of the decade's slow gains in youth participation were erased when the percentage of 18–24-year-olds who participated in elections dropped to 37% in 2024, a level that had not been seen since 2015. During that period of time, the participation rate of individuals aged 25–34 years decreased from 55% to 41%. gap that is growing, not a new one


The analysis conducted by IPPR at the constituency level reveals that voter participation was closely associated with factors such as wealth, home ownership, and age at the neighborhood level, not just on a national scale. Constituencies that had a greater number of residents who were older, homeowners, and settled were able to surpass the national figure, whereas those that had populations that were younger and more transient would not have qualified. Ipsos found that C2DEs had a turnout rate of 47%, while ABs had a turnout rate of 67%, and renters had a turnout rate of 38%, while owner-occupiers had a turnout rate of 71% as compared to renters.

Beyond the number that is featured in the headlines, the significance


It is possible to create a Parliament that is structurally more responsive to those who consistently show up if younger voters are not adequately represented. In practice, if not always out loud, politicians of all parties are aware that an older homeowner is a safer electoral bet to court than a 22-year-old renter. This is not because the concerns of the former group are less important than those of the latter group; rather, it is because one group votes more reliably than the other. Over the course of multiple Parliaments, asymmetry can be used to determine which issues receive sustained political attention and which are merely optional decisions.


Therefore, researchers should avoid drawing conclusions based on a single election because there has not been a single cause that has been identified for the decline. Ipsos notes that survey-based turnout estimates have a larger margin of error than headline vote-share figures. This is due to the fact that people who are politically engaged are more likely to respond to post-election polling. Nevertheless, the research is characterized by a number of recurring themes. Due to the fact that younger voters move more frequently and are more likely to fall off the electoral register between elections, the International Political and Political Research (IPPR) recommends specifically automatic or assisted voter registration. The removal of this administrative barrier is utilized by a number of democracies that are comparable. There is a lack of routine among younger voters, which is what keeps older voters coming back to the polls. The fact that leaflets, local press, and traditional broadcast debate formats were not designed for a significant portion of younger voters is something that this series brings up again and again in its examination of digital campaigning.


Interpretation of the data A single election cycle resulted in a ten percentage point increase in the number of voters aged 18 to 24. When it comes to moving a volatile demographic in this manner, voter contact strategies that are based on the final months of a campaign are beyond the point of no return. Rather than calling for a rewritten script closer to election day, the evidence suggests that sustained and credible engagement should take place long before a contest is called.


Data from registrations adds depth to the age narrative. Prior to a general election that took place a few weeks later, the Electoral Commission estimated in April 2024 that seven million eligible voters in England and Wales were either absent, incomplete, or registered at the incorrect address. As is the case with low turnout, under-registration is over-represented among younger people, private renters, and recent movers. This is due to the fact that the annual household canvass and postal registration system assumes address stability, which is something that a 22-year-old renter is much less likely to have than a 68-year-old homeowner. It is impossible for a voter to cast a ballot if they are not properly registered, regardless of whether or not they intend to vote. Therefore, a portion of the age gap in turnout figures is due to a registration gap rather than a motivation gap.


Turnout is not a fixed fact of political life, which is something that anyone who is concerned about British democracy rather than party fortunes should bring to their attention. Within a single cycle, it moved ten points among individuals aged 18 to 24. If the manner in which, when, and by whom it is asked to vote of a cohort that is disengaged in one election changes, then that cohort may change its participation in the subsequent election.
